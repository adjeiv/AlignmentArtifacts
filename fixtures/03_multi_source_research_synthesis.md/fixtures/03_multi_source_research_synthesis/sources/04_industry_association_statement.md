# Source 4 — Industry association statement

**Publisher:** EuroCellPower (European battery and energy storage
manufacturers' association)
**Title:** "Statement on Implementation Timelines for the EU Battery
Regulation"
**Published:** 12 February 2026
**URL (as accessed):** eurocellpower.eu/positions/battery-regulation-implementation-2026

---

EuroCellPower represents manufacturers of portable, industrial, LMT and
EV batteries across seventeen Member States. We welcome the harmonised
framework the Regulation provides, but our members continue to raise
concrete implementation concerns that we believe merit the Commission's
attention before the next milestones fall due.

**On carbon footprint methodology.** Our members cannot finalise
declaration processes for the 18 February 2025 date named in Article 7
without the calculation methodology the Article itself requires the
Commission to adopt. We note this methodology remains outstanding and
renew our call for a formal, published extension rather than an
unpublished de facto delay, for legal certainty.

**On due diligence.** The 18 August 2025 due diligence obligation is now
in force and our members have, in the main, integrated it into supplier
qualification processes. We do flag that guidance on what constitutes an
adequate due diligence policy for smaller (non-exempt) operators remains
thin.

**On recycled content minimums.** We consider the Article 8 minimum
percentages due in 2031 — particularly the 16% cobalt and 6% lithium
figures — to be based on recycled-material availability assumptions that
current recycling capacity in the Union cannot support on that timeline.
We have formally requested that the Commission's mandatory pre-2031 review
of these figures include a capacity assessment, and that the review
process allow for downward revision if capacity targets are not met. This
is, to be clear, a request under the Regulation's existing review
mechanism — no change to the 2031 date or the percentages themselves has
been made or proposed by the Commission at this time.

**On removability/replaceability.** We support the policy objective of
Article 11 but have asked the Commission to consider additional exemption
categories (beyond those currently listed for devices requiring a
continuous power supply or intended for use in humid or submerged
conditions) ahead of the 18 February 2027 application date, covering
certain compact medical and safety-critical devices.

We will continue to update members as the Commission responds to these
requests.
