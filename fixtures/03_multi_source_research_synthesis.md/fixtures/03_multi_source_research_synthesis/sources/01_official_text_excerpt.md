Parliament and of the Council of 12 July 2023 concerning batteries and

## Scope (Article 1–2)

The Regulation applies to all batteries placed on the market or put into
service in the Union, regardless of origin: portable batteries, batteries
for light means of transport (LMT — e.g. e-bikes, e-scooters), starting,
lighting and ignition (SLI) batteries, electric vehicle (EV) batteries,
and industrial batteries. It replaces Directive 2006/66/EC on a phased
basis rather than a single cut-over date.

## Key substantive requirements and their application dates

**Restricted substances (Article 6, Annex I).** Existing restrictions
carried over from Directive 2006/66/EC (cadmium, mercury) continued
without a gap; additional restrictions apply from **18 August 2024**.

**Carbon footprint declaration — EV batteries (Article 7).** Manufacturers
must accompany each EV battery model with a carbon footprint declaration
from **18 February 2025**, calculated per a methodology to be set out in a
delegated act under Article 7(1). Carbon footprint **performance classes**
apply from **18 August 2026**, and a **maximum carbon footprint threshold**
applies from a date to be fixed once the Commission adopts the
corresponding delegated act — the Regulation's own text describes this
third stage as conditional on that act's adoption, without a fixed
calendar date.

**Due diligence policy (Article 48).** Economic operators placing batteries
on the market, other than SMEs, must establish, operate and maintain a
battery due diligence policy addressing the sourcing of cobalt, natural
graphite, lithium, nickel and related chemical compounds, from **18 August
2025**.

**Labelling (Article 13, Annex VI).** General labelling — capacity,
chemistry, presence of hazardous substances, separate-collection symbol —
applies from **18 August 2026**. A **QR code** linking to further
information (the precursor to the full digital passport) applies from
**18 August 2027**.

**Removability and replaceability — portable batteries (Article 11).**
Appliances must be designed so that portable batteries incorporated in
them can be readily removed and replaced by the end-user, from **18
February 2027**, subject to listed exemptions (e.g. devices requiring a
continuous power supply, or designed for use in wet environments).

**Electronic battery passport (Article 77).** Required for LMT batteries,
industrial batteries with capacity above 2 kWh, and EV batteries, from
**18 February 2027**.

**Minimum recycled content (Article 8, Annex II).** Manufacturers must
declare the share of recycled cobalt, lead, lithium and nickel in each
battery model from **18 August 2028**. Mandatory **minimum percentages**
(cobalt 16%, lead 85%, lithium 6%, nickel 6%) apply from **18 August
2031**, subject to review and possible revision by delegated act before
that date.

**Collection targets — portable batteries (Article 59).** Minimum
collection rate of **63% by 31 December 2027**, rising to **73% by 31
December 2030**. A separate LMT battery collection target applies:
**51% by 31 December 2028**, **61% by 31 December 2031**.

**Recycling efficiency / material recovery (Article 71, Annex XII).**
Lithium recovery from waste batteries: **50% by 31 December 2027**, **80%
by 31 December 2031**.

## Note on conditional dates

Several of the dates above are stated in the Regulation as applying
"subject to the entry into force of the implementing/delegated act referred
to in" the relevant article. As of this excerpt, multiple such acts —
notably the Article 7(1) carbon-footprint calculation methodology — had
not yet been adopted by the Commission, meaning the practical start of
some obligations may lag the calendar date stated in the Regulation's own
text. This distinction between a statutory date and a practically
enforceable date recurs across secondary sources and is a frequent source
of disagreement between them.
