# Clippings Digest — Renewable Energy, compiled by intern rotation
(Internal use only — this is us skimming other people's newsletters and industry blogs and jotting down what stood out, not original reporting. Do not cite this document itself and do not lift its wording — go find whatever it's referring to.)

---

**Solar tech** — Saw multiple newsletters mention a new tandem cell efficiency record again, somewhere in the low-to-mid 30s percent range, attributed to "a research consortium." A couple of the write-ups didn't even agree on which institution. Also several mentions of thin-film and perovskite manufacturers signing pilot-line deals, framed pretty breathlessly ("perovskite's commercial breakthrough is finally here") in a way that reads more like a press-release rewrite than independent reporting.

**Wind** — General sense from skimming is that offshore wind in the US has had a rough stretch — permitting uncertainty, a couple of projects reportedly cancelled or downsized, financing getting harder. One post claimed a specific project was "dead" that another post two weeks later said had resumed construction, so treat any single claim here as unreliable without checking the primary source and its date. Floating offshore wind pilot projects in Europe and Asia got a few mentions too, described vaguely as "gaining momentum."

**Storage** — Lots of chatter about battery costs continuing to fall and long-duration storage (iron-air, flow batteries, compressed air) getting more pilot deployments. Sodium-ion batteries came up in a few places as a lower-cost alternative to lithium-ion for stationary storage, with at least one mention of a large-scale deployment in China, but the digest entries don't agree on scale (MWh figures ranged from "pilot" to "utility-scale" depending on which newsletter you read).

**Grids and transmission** — Several outlets covering the idea that grid interconnection queues and permitting, not generation hardware, are now the main bottleneck to renewables deployment in both the US and EU. Some mention of policy responses — streamlined permitting proposals, interconnection queue reform — but framed at a high level without much specificity on what actually passed versus what's still proposed.

**Policy** — Mixed bag: some newsletters describe continued US federal-level rollback or uncertainty around clean energy tax credits, while others describe individual states pushing ahead with their own targets regardless. EU coverage leaned toward grid/interconnection and permitting reform rather than new subsidy programs. A couple of items on countries in Asia and the Middle East announcing large utility-scale solar-plus-storage tenders with aggressively low winning bid prices, but exact figures vary by source and none of the digest entries name the actual auction authority.

**Manufacturing/supply chain** — Recurring theme of Chinese solar module oversupply pushing prices down globally, with knock-on effects for manufacturers elsewhere (some Western and Indian manufacturers reportedly struggling to compete on price, a few citing plant slowdowns). Framed alternately as good news (cheaper deployment) and bad news (non-Chinese manufacturing base under pressure) depending on the outlet's angle.

**Hydrogen** — General tone across clippings is more cautious/negative than a year ago — mentions of delayed final investment decisions, project cancellations or rescoping, and skepticism about near-term green hydrogen cost competitiveness, but again no consistent specifics on which projects or by how much.

---

*Compiled from skimming ~15 different industry newsletters and blogs over a couple of weeks; not verified against original sources; several items above are internally inconsistent between newsletters. Treat as a "what's being talked about" pointer, not a citable fact set.*
