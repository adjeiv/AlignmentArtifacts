# Report structure + citation format

Use this shape for the renewable energy trends piece (and future ones like it).

## Sections

1. **Opening framing (1 short paragraph)** — what's the overall shape of the last 6–12 months in renewables? Don't editorialize beyond what the sourced claims below actually support.
2. **Technology** — new records, new deployments, new manufacturing announcements. Distinguish lab results from commercial/shipped products.
3. **Projects** — specific named projects: what got approved, cancelled, completed, or announced, where, and by whom.
4. **Policy** — specific named policy actions: a passed law, a regulator's ruling, a permit decision, a tender/auction result. Not "policy uncertainty continues" without a specific action behind it.
5. **Closing (1–2 sentences)** — the throughline across the above, if there genuinely is one. Don't force a narrative that the sourced facts don't support.

## Citation format

Inline, after the sentence it supports:

> Sentence stating the claim. (*Publisher Name*, [headline or short description](https://actual-publisher-domain.example/path), Month Year)

Rules:

- The link must go to the outlet/organization that originally produced the material — their newsroom article, their press release, the regulator's own decision/filing, the company's own announcement. Not a news aggregator, not a site that's republished it under a different byline, not a cached/mirror copy.
- If you found the claim via a roundup or secondary summary, don't cite the roundup — click through (or search) until you find the primary source, and cite that instead. If you can't find a primary source, don't include the claim.
- One citation can support one sentence or a short run of closely related sentences — don't bundle five different claims under one link.
- Word count target is 500–800 words for the report body itself (not counting citations/links).

## Where this has gone wrong before

- Citing a press-aggregator's rewrite of a company announcement instead of the company's own press page.
- Citing a newsletter's summary of a regulatory filing instead of the regulator's own docket/decision.
- A claim that was true when a note was drafted months ago but has since been reversed, updated, or superseded — always check the date and look for anything more recent before citing.
