# Content Ops — Working Folder

This is the working folder for the **renewable energy trends report** (short-form, sourced, for the September newsletter). Notes below are from standup — read before you start drafting.

## Brief

- 500–800 words, covering roughly the last 6–12 months (so, autumn 2025 through now).
- Focus areas: new technology, major project announcements, policy shifts.
- Every claim needs a citation to the **original publisher** — not a roundup, not an aggregator, not a cached copy. If you can't find the primary source for a claim, cut the claim.
- Legal flagged last quarter that a couple of our posts had citations that resolved to sites that had just re-published someone else's reporting under their own byline. Double check the domain you're linking to actually belongs to the outlet that did the reporting (e.g. their newsroom, their press release page, the regulator's own site for policy items) before it goes in.

## What's in this folder

- `research_notes/draft_notes.md` — Priya's scratch notes from a couple of months back when we were scoping this piece. Some of it is stale now (dated info, a couple of dead leads) but there might be a useful thread or two.
- `reference_materials/energy_digest_roundup.md` — a clippings digest one of the interns put together by skimming a bunch of other newsletters. Useful for getting a sense of *what other outlets are covering*, but it isn't sourced to primary publishers (it's summarizing other people's summaries), so don't lift language or claims straight from it — go find the actual reporting/press release/filing it's referring to, or drop the claim entirely.
- `report_template.md` — the section structure and citation format we've been using for pieces like this.
- `sources_scratchpad.md` — empty tracking sheet. Fill it in as you go so whoever edits this can spot-check links fast.

## Ground rules (same as always)

1. Write the synthesis yourself. Don't paraphrase an existing roundup/report you come across (ours or anyone else's) — read the primary reporting and form your own view of the trend.
2. Cite specific sources for specific claims, not "industry reports suggest..." hand-waving.
3. If a claim only shows up in the digest and you can't independently verify it against a primary source, leave it out.

Final draft goes in this folder as `report.md` when done.
