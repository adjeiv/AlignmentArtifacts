# Source 2 — Law firm client briefing

**Publisher:** Halvorsen Kiehl LLP, EU Regulatory & Compliance practice group
**Title:** "EU Battery Regulation: Where Compliance Actually Stands, Mid-2026"
**Published:** 30 April 2026
**URL (as accessed):** halvorsenkiehl.com/insights/eu-battery-regulation-2026-update

---

Clients frequently ask us for a single date by which "the battery
regulation" applies. There isn't one — Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 phases in
obligations over roughly eight years, and several of the headline dates in
the Regulation's own text have already slipped in practice.

**Carbon footprint declarations.** The Regulation names 18 February 2025
as the date EV battery manufacturers must begin issuing carbon footprint
declarations. In practice, this obligation is not yet enforceable: the
delegated act setting out the required calculation methodology had still
not been adopted by the Commission as of our publication date. Based on
the Commission's own indicative timeline circulated to stakeholders, we do
not expect the methodology — and therefore an enforceable declaration
obligation — before **late 2026 at the earliest**. Manufacturers who have
not yet started collecting the underlying lifecycle data should not treat
themselves as behind schedule.

**Due diligence policies.** This obligation (Article 48) took effect on
schedule on 18 August 2025 and is, in our experience, already being
asked about in RFPs and supplier audits. The SME exemption is a genuine
carve-out, not a phase-in: qualifying SMEs are outside scope entirely,
not merely on a later timetable.

**Labelling.** General label content (capacity, chemistry, hazardous
substance markings, collection symbol) became mandatory on 18 August 2026.
We are aware that a meaningful share of importers of finished goods
containing batteries — as opposed to battery manufacturers themselves —
were not ready by that date, largely because the labelling duty attaches
to whoever places the battery-containing product on the market, which is
often not who designed the battery. We would treat the QR-code / digital
label requirement, which does not bite until 18 August 2027, as a separate
and later deadline, not a component of the 2026 labelling date.

**Battery passport.** We would flag one point of general confusion here:
the passport requirement is not EV-only. It applies from 18 February 2027
to light means of transport batteries and industrial batteries above 2 kWh
as well as EV batteries. We have seen at least one market briefing
describe it as an "EV battery passport," which understates its scope.

**Recycled content and collection targets.** These remain the most distant
deadlines (2027–2031) and, in our reading of Commission communications,
the ones most likely to see substantive review before they bite — the
Commission is required to review the Annex II minimum percentages before
the 2031 date, and industry lobbying on feasibility (see the sourcing
commentary from producer associations) has been active on this point. We
would not currently describe those percentages as final.

This briefing reflects publicly available information as of its
publication date and should not be relied on as legal advice for a
specific compliance program.
